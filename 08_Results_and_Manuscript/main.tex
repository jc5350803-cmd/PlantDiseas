\begin{abstract}
Reliable automated plant disease recognition requires more than high-capacity convolutional feature extractors. A practical model must capture disease cues at different spatial scales, retain useful low-level evidence as depth increases, and avoid unnecessary channel growth. This study presents PlantDiseaseNet-Hybrid, a custom convolutional architecture that deliberately combines two complementary fusion operations. Parallel $3\times3$, $5\times5$, and $7\times7$ convolutions are fused by element-wise addition in the Inception block, keeping representation width fixed while combining local and broader texture responses. A subsequent $7\times7$ convolution with batch normalization is connected to its input by channel-wise concatenation, allowing earlier features to remain directly available to later layers. Thus, addition is used for compact multi-scale fusion, whereas concatenation is used for information preservation and feature reuse.

The study is organized as three linked investigations. First, the hybrid network is evaluated separately on Wheat, Cotton, and PlantVillage data. Second, a controlled combined-dataset augmentation study compares a no-augmentation control with strong and light augmentation strategies. Third, architecture reference experiments isolate Standard Inception, residual addition, Inception(Add), and SkipConnection(Concat). A separate soybean experiment is retained as a robustness/transferability study rather than being mixed with the 24-class primary benchmark. Repository inspection was based on saved notebook outputs and result files; notebooks were not re-executed for this manuscript. The verified primary runs record 97.81\% accuracy for Wheat, 53.55\% for Cotton, and 97.67\% for PlantVillage. On the combined benchmark, the augmentation study records 96.03\% without augmentation, 96.31\% with strong augmentation, and 97.06\% with light augmentation. The complete hybrid architecture without augmentation records 95.91\% accuracy with 934,200 parameters. These results show both the potential of the hybrid feature-fusion strategy and the importance of reporting dataset and augmentation effects separately.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Plant diseases are a persistent constraint on agricultural productivity because infection can alter leaf colour, texture, venation, lesion geometry, and other visual characteristics before the effect becomes obvious at the crop level. Image-based diagnosis offers a scalable alternative to repeated manual inspection, but reliability depends strongly on the quality and diversity of the visual representation learned by the classifier. Public plant-image benchmarks have demonstrated that convolutional networks can recognize many disease categories, while also showing the distinction between performance on curated imagery and robustness to changes in acquisition conditions \citep{mohanty2016using,sladojevic2016deep}.

A central architectural question is how features extracted at different scales should be fused. Inception-style networks use parallel convolutions with different receptive fields, while residual networks use addition to simplify optimization and DenseNet uses concatenation to promote feature reuse \citep{szegedy2015going,he2016deep,huang2017densely}. Each choice has a cost. Concatenating all multi-scale branches preserves their responses but increases channel width, whereas adding branches keeps the tensor compact but does not preserve each branch independently. PlantDiseaseNet-Hybrid assigns these operations different roles rather than treating them as interchangeable.

The proposed Inception(Add) block uses three convolutional branches with $3\times3$, $5\times5$, and $7\times7$ kernels and adds their outputs element-wise. Because the branch widths are equal, the output width remains fixed. The subsequent SkipConnection(Concat) block applies a $7\times7$ convolution and batch normalization and concatenates the transformed features with the incoming representation. This combination provides compact multi-scale fusion followed by explicit feature preservation.

Data augmentation is treated as an experimental factor rather than merely as a preprocessing sentence. The combined-dataset experiments compare a no-augmentation control with stronger and lighter perturbation policies. This separation matters because an apparent architecture gain can otherwise be confounded by a change in the training distribution. The resulting analysis also tests whether more aggressive augmentation is necessarily preferable.

The main contributions are: (i) a hybrid addition--concatenation CNN for multi-scale disease representation and feature preservation; (ii) an explicit separation of architecture and augmentation studies; (iii) crop-wise primary evaluations on Wheat, Cotton, and PlantVillage; (iv) a controlled combined-dataset augmentation comparison; and (v) a separate soybean robustness study.

\section{Related Work}
CNN-based plant disease recognition has progressed from task-specific networks to transfer-learning and deeper residual/dense architectures. Mohanty \textit{et al.} demonstrated large-scale image-based plant disease classification using PlantVillage imagery, while Sladojevic \textit{et al.} investigated deep neural networks for leaf disease recognition \citep{mohanty2016using,sladojevic2016deep}. These studies established the value of learned visual representations while also making dataset composition an important consideration.

Inception introduced parallel convolutional paths with different receptive fields for multi-scale representation learning \citep{szegedy2015going}. ResNet showed that residual identity mappings can ease optimization of deep networks \citep{he2016deep}. DenseNet extended feature reuse by concatenating preceding feature maps \citep{huang2017densely}. The proposed model draws on these complementary principles but uses addition specifically to control multi-scale channel growth and concatenation specifically to preserve incoming features.

Image augmentation is widely used to reduce overfitting and expose classifiers to plausible appearance variation. The effectiveness of a transformation depends on whether it preserves task semantics; therefore, this study measures augmentation policies explicitly instead of assuming that stronger perturbation is always beneficial \citep{shorten2019survey}.

\section{Materials and Methods}
\subsection{Datasets and Experimental Organization}
The project contains experiments using PlantVillage-derived imagery, Wheat Disease data, Cotton Disease data, and a separate soybean dataset. The principal multi-crop benchmark contains 24 disease categories. Crop-specific experiments are retained as the primary study because they test the same hybrid architecture under different class distributions. The combined experiments then address architecture and augmentation under a common multi-crop setting.

Several duplicate, exploratory, and incomplete notebooks exist in the repository. Numerical claims in this manuscript are restricted to executed notebooks or saved result artifacts that can be traced to the corresponding experiment. Unexecuted copies are not used as evidence.

\subsection{Preprocessing}
Images are represented as RGB inputs and resized to $256\times256$ pixels before model training. Pixel intensities are normalized. The primary workflow records a training/validation/test organization, while individual experimental notebooks are treated as authoritative for their exact split. The datasets are public image collections; consequently, results are not interpreted as proof of performance on unrestricted field imagery.

\subsection{Strategic Data Augmentation}
The recorded augmentation operations include horizontal flipping, rotation, zooming, brightness adjustment, translation, and contrast modification. The combined study distinguishes strong and light policies. The strong policy introduces broader geometric and appearance perturbations, whereas the light policy uses milder changes. The purpose is not simply to enlarge the training set, but to examine whether transformations remain useful without obscuring disease-relevant structure.

\subsection{PlantDiseaseNet-Hybrid Architecture}
The network receives a $256\times256\times3$ RGB image. Two Inception(Add) blocks are followed by two SkipConnection(Concat) blocks, with four max-pooling stages reducing spatial resolution. Global average pooling converts the final tensor into a compact vector, followed by dense layers of 512 and 128 units and a softmax output.

\begin{figure*}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.96\textwidth]{overall_architecture.png}
\caption{Overall architecture of PlantDiseaseNet-Hybrid. Compact multi-scale fusion is followed by feature-preserving concatenation before global average pooling and classification.}
\label{fig:architecture}
\end{figure*}

\subsubsection{Inception(Add) block}
For an input tensor $X$, the three branch responses are fused as
\begin{equation}
Y=F_{3\times3}(X)+F_{5\times5}(X)+F_{7\times7}(X).
\end{equation}
Element-wise addition keeps the output channel count equal to the branch width. The main computational motivation is therefore to obtain multi-scale responses without the immediate channel multiplication caused by concatenating three branches. The trade-off is that the branch-specific responses are blended before later layers can access them independently.

\begin{figure}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.96\columnwidth]{inception_block.jpg}
\caption{Proposed Inception(Add) block.}
\label{fig:inception}
\end{figure}

\subsubsection{SkipConnection(Concat) block}
The skip block performs a $7\times7$ convolution followed by batch normalization and then concatenates the transformed representation with its input:
\begin{equation}
Y=\operatorname{Concat}\left(X,F(X)\right).
\end{equation}
This increases channel depth, but preserves the incoming representation explicitly. The design is intended to reduce the need for deeper layers to reconstruct information that may remain useful for fine-grained disease discrimination.

\end{figure}

\subsection{Model Configuration}
The main implementation uses Adam optimization with a learning rate of 0.001, batch size 32, up to 60 epochs, categorical cross-entropy, cosine warmup annealing, and early stopping with patience 12. Dropout is used in the classifier. The displayed Keras implementation contains two Inception cells, two skip cells, four pooling stages, global average pooling, dense layers of 512 and 128 units, and a 24-unit softmax classifier.

\section{Experimental Studies}
\subsection{Primary Crop-wise Experiments}
The primary study comprises three crop-specific hybrid experiments: Wheat, Cotton, and PlantVillage. This design avoids hiding dataset dependence behind one pooled score. The same architectural concept is therefore examined under different disease distributions and levels of class difficulty.

\label{tab:primary}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrrr}
\toprule
Dataset & Acc. & Prec. & Rec. & F1 & Spec. \\
\midrule
Wheat & 97.81 & 97.82 & 97.81 & 97.81 & 98.54 \\
Cotton & 53.55 & 53.01 & 53.55 & 51.66 & 90.71 \\
PlantVillage & 97.67 & 97.68 & 97.67 & 97.68 & 99.83 \\
\bottomrule

The large difference between the Cotton run and the Wheat/PlantVillage runs is not treated as an error to be hidden. It is evidence that architecture alone does not determine recognition difficulty. Dataset composition, class separability, image quality, and disease appearance can materially affect performance.

\subsection{Combined-Dataset Augmentation Study}
The augmentation experiment holds the combined 24-class task constant and varies the training augmentation policy. The no-augmentation control records 96.03\% accuracy. Strong augmentation reaches 96.31\%, whereas the light policy reaches 97.06\%. Relative to the no-augmentation control, the gains are therefore 0.28 and 1.03 percentage points, respectively.

\begin{table}[!t]
\centering

\label{tab:augresults}

\toprule

\midrule
No augmentation & 96.03\% & -- & -- \\
Strong augmentation & 96.31\% & 99.84\% & 0.9990 \\

The pattern is more informative than the absolute gain alone. The stronger policy does not outperform the lighter policy, despite introducing greater variation. A plausible interpretation is that mild perturbation improves invariance to acquisition differences while retaining disease-relevant colour, texture, and lesion geometry. This is an empirical interpretation rather than a claim that light augmentation is universally optimal.

\subsection{Architecture Study}
The architecture study isolates four references on the combined benchmark: Standard Inception with branch concatenation, a ResNet-style residual architecture, Inception(Add) alone, and SkipConnection(Concat) alone. These are architecture comparisons, not dataset-based ablations.

The isolated variants show that neither mechanism alone should be presented as the sole source of performance. Inception(Add) remains competitive while keeping multi-scale fusion compact, whereas SkipConnection(Concat) preserves the incoming representation. The complete hybrid model without augmentation records 95.91\% accuracy with 934,200 parameters. The 97.06\% light-augmentation result is reported separately because it changes the training distribution and therefore is not a pure architecture ablation.

\subsection{Soybean Robustness Study}
The soybean experiments are retained as a separate study because the dataset and configuration differ from the main 24-class benchmark. The executed augmented soybean run records perfect test metrics in its saved output. This result is treated as a robustness/transferability observation, not as evidence that the main 24-class model universally achieves perfect recognition. The soybean result is consequently excluded from the primary ranking table.

\section{Results and Discussion}
The combined evidence supports a nuanced conclusion. The hybrid architecture performs strongly on Wheat and PlantVillage but is much less effective on the recorded Cotton experiment. This demonstrates why a journal study should report crop-specific behaviour instead of relying on a single aggregate number. The architecture is a feature-fusion mechanism; it cannot eliminate differences in disease appearance or dataset difficulty.

The architecture study provides a useful mechanistic perspective. Standard Inception preserves branch outputs through concatenation but pays a channel-growth cost. Inception(Add) reduces this growth and remains competitive. SkipConnection(Concat) preserves earlier information but does not itself supply explicit multi-scale branch fusion. The complete model therefore combines two complementary behaviours rather than simply selecting one fusion operation.

The augmentation study provides an independent line of evidence. The light policy improves the combined score by 1.03 percentage points over the no-augmentation control, while the stronger policy improves it by only 0.28 points. The result suggests that augmentation strength should be tuned to the visual semantics of the task. Excessive perturbation can change precisely the colour, texture, boundary, and spatial relationships that a classifier must learn.

The parameter count for the displayed Keras model is 934,200. This value is obtained by summing the parameter counts shown in the model summary and is used instead of the earlier 933,426 figure. The discrepancy should not be concealed in a final submission: the model summary is the appropriate source of truth for the implemented configuration.

Several limitations remain. The public datasets are not equivalent to unconstrained field imagery. The Cotton result indicates dataset sensitivity. The soybean study is based on a different experimental setting and should not be merged with the primary benchmark. Finally, because the architecture and augmentation studies were designed as separate experiments, their interaction has not been estimated by a full factorial design. Future experiments should train every architecture variant under identical augmentation policies before making causal claims about architecture--augmentation interaction.

\section{Conclusion}
PlantDiseaseNet-Hybrid combines compact multi-scale addition with feature-preserving concatenation. Addition is used inside the Inception block to blend responses from multiple receptive fields without immediate channel multiplication, while concatenation is used in the skip block to retain incoming information alongside newly transformed features.

The experimental structure is as important as the architecture. Crop-wise primary experiments, controlled augmentation comparisons, architecture references, and the separate soybean study answer different scientific questions and are therefore reported separately. The verified results show high performance on Wheat and PlantVillage, substantially lower performance on Cotton, and a measurable benefit from the lighter augmentation policy on the combined benchmark. These findings support the hybrid fusion strategy while also demonstrating that dataset composition and training distribution must remain explicit in any claim of robustness.

Future work should evaluate the model on genuinely field-collected imagery, conduct a matched factorial architecture--augmentation study, assess calibration and confidence, and investigate attention-based, explainable, and edge-deployment extensions.
