\begin{abstract}
Plant disease recognition from leaf images is a useful computer-vision problem, but a high score on one controlled dataset is not sufficient evidence of a robust agricultural classifier. Disease symptoms may occur at different spatial scales, visually similar classes may share colour and texture cues, and repeated widening of feature tensors can increase the cost of multi-scale networks. This work presents \emph{PlantDiseaseNet-Hybrid}, a custom convolutional framework built around two deliberately different feature-fusion operations. Its Inception(Add) cells process the same input with $3\times3$, $5\times5$, and $7\times7$ convolutions and combine the branch responses by element-wise addition. This keeps the output channel width fixed and limits the channel growth that would result from concatenating all three branches. The subsequent SkipConnection(Concat) cells use a $7\times7$ convolution, batch normalization, and channel-wise concatenation with the incoming representation. This preserves earlier features explicitly while adding newly learned features. The combination is therefore an asymmetric or dual-mode fusion strategy: addition is used for compact multi-scale synthesis, whereas concatenation is used for feature preservation. The study separates dataset-specific evaluation, a controlled architectural ablation, a dedicated augmentation sensitivity study, and an additional Indian soybean evaluation. On the 24-class combined benchmark, the recorded complete hybrid obtains 95.91\% accuracy, 95.98\% precision, 95.91\% recall, 95.91\% F1-score, 99.82\% specificity, 0.9996 micro-AUC and 0.9990 macro-AUC, with 934,200 total parameters. The controlled augmentation study records 97.06\% for the Flip+Brightness configuration, while the five-fold soybean experiment reports 99.57\% $\pm$ 0.85\% mean accuracy. Importantly, the residual comparator reaches 96.04\% on the 24-class benchmark, so the proposed method is not presented as universally superior. Instead, the results support a more specific conclusion: asymmetric fusion provides a competitive accuracy--capacity trade-off, and moderate augmentation can be more effective than indiscriminate augmentation for this dataset.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Plant diseases are an important source of crop loss and quality degradation. Their visible symptoms can involve changes in colour, texture, lesion shape, venation, or the spatial distribution of damaged tissue. Conventional identification by farmers, extension personnel, or plant pathologists remains indispensable, but it is difficult to scale when large numbers of plants must be screened repeatedly. Image-based deep learning provides a complementary approach in which discriminative visual representations are learned directly from labelled images.

The PlantVillage work of Mohanty et al.~\citep{mohanty2016} established an influential benchmark for this problem. Using 54,306 images representing 14 crop species and 26 diseases or healthy classes, the study demonstrated 99.35\% accuracy under controlled acquisition conditions. Later studies continued to report very high scores on PlantVillage, including DenseNet and EfficientNet based approaches~\citep{too2019,atila2021}. These results are valuable, but controlled-dataset accuracy and robustness to heterogeneous crop imagery are not equivalent properties.

A second issue concerns the scale of disease evidence. Small lesions, vein-level changes, powdery regions, and broad discoloration can occur simultaneously. Inception-style architectures address this by applying different receptive fields in parallel~\citep{szegedy2015}. Standard Inception concatenates branch outputs, which preserves branch-specific features but increases channel width. PlantDiseaseNet-Hybrid instead uses element-wise addition in its Inception cells. Because all branches have the same number of filters and spatial dimensions, the fused representation has the same channel width as one branch. The operation is therefore intended as a compact multi-scale synthesis mechanism.

Feature preservation presents a different requirement. As the network becomes deeper and spatial resolution is reduced, useful lower-level information can be transformed or discarded. Residual networks address optimisation and information flow through additive shortcuts~\citep{he2016}, whereas DenseNet explicitly concatenates earlier and newly generated features~\citep{huang2017}. PlantDiseaseNet-Hybrid adopts the latter principle for its skip stages: the original input is retained alongside the newly computed representation. Consequently, the network does not apply addition and concatenation interchangeably. Addition is used where branch responses should be compressed into one multi-scale representation; concatenation is used where earlier information should remain explicitly accessible.

This asymmetric use of the two operations is the central design hypothesis. The study therefore evaluates four separate questions: how the hybrid behaves across crop datasets; what changes when its components are removed or replaced; how sensitive the complete model is to augmentation policy; and whether it remains useful on an additional soybean dataset with pronounced class imbalance. Treating these questions as separate experimental families distinguishes architectural effects from dataset effects and augmentation effects.

The contributions are: (i) a custom PlantDiseaseNet-Hybrid architecture combining Inception(Add) and SkipConnection(Concat); (ii) an explicit rationale for assigning different fusion operators to different stages; (iii) crop-specific and combined-dataset evaluation; (iv) a controlled architectural comparison; (v) a separate augmentation sensitivity study; and (vi) a soybean evaluation in which cross-validation is used to provide a more credible estimate than a single split in the presence of a very small minority class.

\section{Related Work}
Deep convolutional networks are widely used for plant disease image classification. Mohanty et al.~\citep{mohanty2016} showed that deep learning can achieve near-ceiling performance on controlled PlantVillage imagery. Too et al.~\citep{too2019} compared VGG, Inception, ResNet and DenseNet families and reported 99.75\% for DenseNet on their PlantVillage evaluation. Atila et al.~\citep{atila2021} evaluated EfficientNet and other architectures and reported accuracies above 99\% for their strongest configurations. These studies motivate strong baselines while also making computational efficiency and dataset composition important considerations.

Inception introduced parallel receptive fields~\citep{szegedy2015}; ResNet demonstrated the value of residual addition~\citep{he2016}; and DenseNet demonstrated explicit feature reuse through concatenation~\citep{huang2017}. The present work combines these principles asymmetrically rather than treating them as interchangeable. The specific hypothesis tested is that addition is useful for compact multi-scale fusion, whereas concatenation is useful for feature-preserving skips.

Crop-specific literature motivates evaluation beyond PlantVillage. Fang et al.~\citep{fang2023} developed a lightweight multiscale CNN for wheat disease detection, while Bishshash et al.~\citep{cotton2024} introduced a field-collected cotton disease dataset. These datasets represent different visual regimes and are therefore useful for testing whether a model's behaviour is dependent on acquisition conditions.

The augmentation study is reported separately because augmentation is an experimental factor rather than merely a preprocessing checkbox. Mhala et al.~\citep{mhala2025} used strategic augmentation with regularisation and transfer learning in a real-world potato disease setting. The present work adopts the reporting principle of comparing augmentation policies under a common architecture; the potato experiment itself is not used as evidence for the results reported here.

\subsection{Image Preprocessing}
All principal image experiments use RGB input resized to $256\times256$ pixels. Pixel intensities are normalised before model input. Resizing establishes common tensor geometry and normalisation reduces numerical-scale variation during optimisation. These operations are kept separate from augmentation so that training-time perturbations can be studied independently.

\subsection{Strategic Data Augmentation}
The recorded augmentation operations include random horizontal flipping, random rotation, zoom transformation, brightness adjustment, translation shifting, and contrast enhancement. Flipping and rotation modify orientation; zoom changes framing; translation introduces spatial displacement; and brightness and contrast changes represent illumination variation.

The dedicated augmentation study compares Hybrid-NoAug, Hybrid-CropColour, Hybrid-LightAug, an independent Hybrid-LightAug-v2 repeat, and Hybrid-FullAug on the same 24-class benchmark. This separation allows the effect of augmentation policy to be distinguished from the generic preprocessing pipeline. The potato augmentation notebook used by Mhala et al.~\citep{mhala2025} is used only as a reporting reference, not as a source of experimental results.

\subsection{PlantDiseaseNet-Hybrid Architecture}
The model receives an RGB tensor of shape $256\times256\times3$. It contains two Inception(Add) cells, four max-pooling stages, two SkipConnection(Concat) blocks, global average pooling, two dense layers with dropout, and a 24-unit softmax output for the combined benchmark. The executed layer sequence is:
\begin{center}
\small Input $\rightarrow$ Inception(Add)-16 $\rightarrow$ BN $\rightarrow$ Pool $\rightarrow$ Inception(Add)-32 $\rightarrow$ BN $\rightarrow$ Pool $\rightarrow$ SkipConcat-64 $\rightarrow$ Pool $\rightarrow$ SkipConcat-128 $\rightarrow$ Pool $\rightarrow$ GAP $\rightarrow$ Dense-512 $\rightarrow$ Dropout $\rightarrow$ Dense-128 $\rightarrow$ Dropout $\rightarrow$ Softmax.
\end{center}

The first Inception cell produces 16 channels at $256\times256$. After pooling, the second produces 32 channels at $128\times128$. The first skip block operates at $64\times64$ and returns 96 channels. The second operates at $32\times32$ and returns 224 channels. A final pooling stage gives $16\times16\times224$, followed by GAP to a 224-dimensional vector.

\widefigure{overall_architecture.png}{0.99}{0.40}{Overall PlantDiseaseNet-Hybrid architecture. Width and height are constrained so that the complete network remains visible.}

\subsubsection{Inception(Add) Block}
Each cell contains parallel $3\times3$, $5\times5$, and $7\times7$ convolutions. Because the branches have compatible dimensions, they are fused element-wise:
\begin{equation}
Y=F_{3\times3}(X)+F_{5\times5}(X)+F_{7\times7}(X).
\end{equation}

Concatenation would carry all three branch outputs forward and increase channel width threefold at the fusion point. Addition retains the width of one branch, reducing intermediate tensor size at high spatial resolutions. The trade-off is that branch-specific responses are not retained as independent channel groups after fusion.

\columnfigure{inception_block.jpg}{0.98}{0.27}{Structure of the proposed Inception(Add) block.}

\subsubsection{SkipConnection(Concat) Block}
The skip block first applies a $7\times7$ convolution and batch normalization, then concatenates the transformed representation with the unmodified input:
\begin{equation}
Y=\operatorname{Concat}(X,F(X)).
\end{equation}

This preserves earlier features explicitly and allows later layers to access both the transformed and original representations. The output depth grows from 32 to 96 channels in the first block and from 96 to 224 channels in the second. The increased width is introduced after spatial downsampling, where feature reuse is prioritised over strict channel minimisation.

\columnfigure{skip_block.jpg}{0.98}{0.27}{Structure of the proposed SkipConnection(Concat) block.}

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}
Accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, specificity and AUC are reported where available. Precision and recall characterise false-positive and false-negative behaviour, F1-score summarises their harmonic mean, and specificity is retained as a class-level measure from the experiment record. Micro- and macro-AUC are reported for multi-class ROC analysis where available.

Aggregate accuracy is not treated as sufficient evidence when class support is highly imbalanced. This is particularly important for the soybean dataset, whose processed subset contains only 10 Root Images.

\subsection{Soybean Evaluation}
\label{sec:soybean}
The soybean study is reported separately because it uses a different published source and has pronounced class imbalance. The processed subset contains 1176 images, including only 10 Root Images. Under such conditions, a random single split can produce extremely small minority-class support.

The experiment record contains two single-split runs with 100\% accuracy and perfect aggregate precision/recall/F1. These values are explicitly marked unreliable because of minority-class support and are not used as the principal soybean result. A light-augmentation run reaches 85.88\%. The more rigorous five-fold configuration using heavy augmentation and a reduced learning rate of $3\times10^{-4}$ reports 99.57\% $\pm$ 0.85\% mean cross-validation accuracy, with a recorded single evaluation of 99.44\% accuracy, 99.5\% precision, 99.4\% recall, 99.4\% F1-score and 99.9\% specificity.

\section{Comparison with Existing Literature}
Direct numerical comparison across papers must be treated cautiously because datasets, class subsets, acquisition conditions, split strategies, and training protocols differ. Mohanty et al.~\citep{mohanty2016} reported 99.35\% on the full controlled PlantVillage benchmark, Too et al.~\citep{too2019} reported 99.75\% for DenseNet in their PlantVillage evaluation, and Atila et al.~\citep{atila2021} reported 99.91--99.97\% for their strongest EfficientNet configurations. These are contextual benchmarks, not directly matched baselines for the 24-class combined experiment.

The present study adds evidence of a different kind: it combines heterogeneous crop sources, changes only architecture in the controlled ablation, and changes only augmentation policy in the sensitivity study. The 95.91\% complete-hybrid result and 97.06\% LightAug result should therefore be interpreted within the specific 24-class benchmark rather than as direct replacements for the full PlantVillage scores in the literature.

\section{Discussion}
\subsection{Why the Hybrid Design is Plausible}
The architecture divides feature fusion into two roles. In the Inception stage, the same input is examined at three receptive-field scales and the responses are compressed by addition. This controls the width of high-resolution tensors. In the skip stage, the objective changes to preserving earlier information, so concatenation retains the original representation alongside newly learned features. The result is a dual-mode feature-fusion strategy rather than a single connectivity rule applied throughout the network.

The ablation supports this interpretation but does not establish universal superiority. The residual baseline achieves 96.04\%, slightly above the hybrid's 95.91\%, while the hybrid uses fewer parameters than the Inception(Add)-only and SkipConcat-only variants. The contribution is therefore best described as a particular accuracy--capacity trade-off.

\subsection{Dataset Difficulty and Cotton}
The large gap between the cotton result and the wheat/PlantVillage results is informative. Cotton reaches 53.55\%, while wheat and the selected PlantVillage subset exceed 97\%. Field-oriented imagery can contain greater background, illumination, occlusion and symptom variability. The implication is that additional network depth may not address the main source of error if domain variation and data coverage are limiting factors.

\subsection{Why Stronger Augmentation is Not Automatically Better}
LightAug reaches 97.06\%, exceeding CropColour at 96.31\% and FullAug at 95.91\%. The independent LightAug-v2 result of 96.43\% also demonstrates run-to-run variability. A plausible interpretation is that moderate perturbations add useful acquisition variation while preserving disease-relevant colour and contrast cues. Stronger transformations may alter those cues. The observation remains specific to this experiment.

\subsection{Soybean and Evaluation Design}
The soybean experiment demonstrates how class imbalance can distort apparent performance. The processed subset contains only 10 Root Images, so a single split can produce unstable minority-class estimates. The two 100\% runs are consequently not used as the headline result. The five-fold estimate of 99.57\% $\pm$ 0.85\% is more informative because it averages over multiple partitions.

\section{Limitations}
The experiments are based on leaf-image datasets and do not constitute an independent field trial of a deployed diagnostic system. Crop-specific datasets differ in class support and acquisition conditions. The wheat run has zero test support for one class, limiting interpretation of its aggregate metrics. The soybean data are strongly imbalanced. The architectural comparison is based on recorded runs rather than a full multi-seed statistical study, so small accuracy differences should not be interpreted as statistically significant without repetition. Finally, inference measurements were obtained on Tesla T4 hardware and cannot be transferred directly to mobile or edge hardware.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
This study presented PlantDiseaseNet-Hybrid, a custom convolutional framework for multi-crop plant disease classification. Its central idea is an asymmetric dual-mode fusion strategy: Inception(Add) cells combine multi-scale responses by addition to control channel width, while SkipConnection(Concat) cells preserve earlier features alongside newly learned features.

The controlled ablation shows that the complete hybrid is competitive with component-only alternatives, although the residual comparator reaches slightly higher raw accuracy. This supports a balanced interpretation of the contribution as an architectural trade-off rather than a claim of universal superiority. The augmentation study shows that moderate augmentation can outperform more aggressive policies, with LightAug reaching 97.06\% on the recorded 24-class benchmark. The soybean analysis further demonstrates why cross-validation is important under severe class imbalance, with the principal five-fold estimate reaching 99.57\% $\pm$ 0.85\%.

Future work should prioritise independent field-data evaluation, repeated multi-seed experiments, calibrated uncertainty, domain adaptation, attention mechanisms for difficult crop-specific classes, and actual edge-device benchmarking after quantisation and pruning.
